SRAM vs. save-states

Two things people both call "saving," and they could not be more different. One is the game's save. The other is a photograph of the entire machine.

TL;DR. SRAM is the game's own battery save — the 3 save slots EarthBound writes when you save at a phone (8 KB, your progress). A save-state is the emulator freezing the whole console at one instant — every byte of RAM, video, audio, and registers (~330 KB). A save-state includes the SRAM, so it's a strict superset: the game's save is just one small slice of the full snapshot.

the game's save

SRAM · 8 KB · .srm

Battery-backed memory the cartridge carries. EarthBound uses it for its 3 save slots — the persistent record of your run.

Written only when you save (call Mom / a phone / a hotel). Survives power-off (that's the battery). It's the "HAL Laboratory, inc." signed block.

Holds: party members & stats, level/EXP, inventory, money in & out of the bank, story flags, current location, playtime — the resumable save file.

In decompbound this is the .srm next to the ROM (plus rotating timestamped backups — dead-cart insurance). The emulator loads it on boot and writes it when the game saves.

the whole machine, frozen

Save-state · ~330 KB · .state

A snapshot of everything, taken at any instant — mid-battle, mid-animation, mid-jump. Not a save point; a save moment.

Written whenever you ask (Ctrl+1–4 / F12). Restoring it drops you back to that exact frame, down to the sub-pixel and the sound envelope.

Holds: all 128 KB WRAM, 64 KB VRAM, palettes (CGRAM), sprites (OAM), every PPU/CPU/DSP register, the 64 KB APU RAM (live audio), and the 8 KB SRAM.

In decompbound this is a slot file — and now, embedded inside a screenshot PNG (drag it onto the window to restore). The save literally rides inside a picture.

Why the distinction matters here

  • A save-state resuming also restores your save file. Because the SRAM is inside it, dropping a save-state back in returns both the live moment and the game's .srm to that point.
  • SRAM is the thing you never want to lose. That's why the emulator keeps rotating, timestamped .srm backups — the specific ghost of a cartridge whose battery never held a save.
  • The state-screenshot feature snapshots the state, not just the SRAM. So a dropped screenshot puts you back mid-anything — far more than a save point. (See the screenshot/drag-drop feature.)

Analogy: SRAM is a bookmark the game let you place in the story. A save-state is a photograph of every atom in the room at one instant — and the bookmark happens to be lying on the desk in that photo.
