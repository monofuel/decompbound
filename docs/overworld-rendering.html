How EarthBound draws the overworld

Why Ness walks in front of a tree trunk but behind its leaves — and how a background can draw on top of a sprite. The answer isn't "more sprites."

TL;DR. The map (ground, trees, buildings) is background tiles, not sprites. Ness, NPCs, and small movable things (mailbox, signs) are sprites (OBJ). A background tile can carry a priority bit that makes it draw in front of sprites. Tree leaves are high-priority background tiles → they cover Ness. Tree trunks are normal (low-priority) tiles → Ness covers them. No extra sprite involved.

The SNES draws in layers

The SNES PPU composites a frame from a few sources, back to front:

  • Background layers (BG1–BG4): grids of 8×8 tiles. EarthBound's overworld map lives here — the grass, roads, houses, and trees are all just tiles arranged on a big scrolling map.
  • Sprites (OBJ): up to 128 free-floating objects, positioned anywhere (not on the tile grid). Ness, NPCs, enemies, and small interactive props are sprites.

Naïvely you'd think "backgrounds are behind, sprites are in front." That's the default — and it's exactly why a broken emulator draws Ness on top of trees. The real hardware is smarter: every tile and every sprite carries a priority, and the PPU interleaves them into one ordered stack.

The trick: a background tile can outrank a sprite

Each tile in a background's tilemap is a 16-bit word. One of those bits — bit 13, the priority bit (mask 0x2000) — splits that layer into two sub-layers:

drawn last · on top
BG tiles with priority bit set high-prio
Tree leaves/canopy, roof edges, awnings, anything you walk behind.
▲ in front of sprites ▲ · · · ▼ behind sprites ▼
the middle
Sprites (OBJ) Ness, NPCs, mailbox
Free-floating objects. Ness sits here, between the two halves of the background.
▲ · · · ▼
drawn first · at the back
BG tiles without priority bit low-prio
Grass, road, tree trunk, the base of a house — the stuff Ness stands in front of.

So the same tree is split across two layers: its trunk tiles are low-priority (behind Ness), its leaf tiles have the priority bit set (in front of Ness). Walk up and your head slides under the canopy — no second sprite, no swap, just two tiles with different priority bits drawn on either side of the sprite layer.

Sprite vs. background: who's who in Onett

Sprites OBJ

  • Ness & party
  • NPCs (townsfolk, the hotel clerk)
  • Enemies wandering the map
  • Small movable/interactive props — a mailbox, a sign, a present box
  • The mushroom that sprouts on Ness's head 🍄 (an overlay sprite)

They move smoothly pixel-by-pixel, independent of the tile grid, and each has its own 2-bit priority slotting it into the stack.

Background BG tiles

  • Grass, dirt, roads, water
  • Buildings (walls, roofs, windows)
  • Trees, bushes, fences
  • Anything that scrolls as one solid map

"That mailbox looks like a sprite" — often it is, precisely because the game wants it to layer with Ness or be interactive. Static scenery stays as cheaper background tiles.

Rule of thumb: if it can move, be talked to, or needs to layer independently with Ness, it's a sprite. If it's fixed scenery, it's a background tile — and if you can walk behind part of it, that part has the priority bit set.

How our emulator does it (and where the bugs were)

In src/decompbound/ppu.nim, bgScanlineInto(…, prio) reads each tilemap word and can filter by that priority bit:

let entry = snes.vram[…]            # 16-bit tilemap word
let tile  = entry and 0x03FF        # which character
if prio >= 0 and ((entry and 0x2000) != 0).int != prio:
  continue                          # skip tiles not in this priority pass

modeLayers then produces the back-to-front passes for the mode — low-priority BG tiles, then sprites, then high-priority BG tiles — so a leaf tile lands in front of Ness while the trunk stays behind. Sprites carry their own 2-bit OBJ priority read from OAM, which interleaves into the same ladder.

Getting this ladder exactly right is fiddly: draw all sprites unconditionally on top and Ness stands in front of every tree; over-correct the interleave and sprites vanish behind a cutscene. Both have been real bugs here — this priority stack is the thing being tuned.
